The most damaging **photo gallery delivery mistakes** are often small: an access code that does not work, a missing download button, unclear selection instructions or a gallery title copied from the previous client.

The photography may be excellent, but delivery is the moment when the client experiences the finished service. If that moment feels confusing or careless, it can weaken the confidence built during the shoot.

A reliable delivery process should answer four questions for the client:

1. How do I open the gallery?
2. What is included?
3. What should I do next?
4. Where can I get help?

This guide explains the common mistakes that prevent those questions from being answered and how to correct them before sending the link.

## Mistake 1: Sending the link without testing it

A photographer can open a gallery successfully while logged into an administrator account, then send a link that does not work for the client.

The client may meet a permission request, an expired password or a page that shows the wrong collection. This immediately turns delivery into a support problem.

Before sending, open the exact client-facing link in a private browser window. Check that:

- the page opens without your photographer account
- the password works
- the correct client and assignment appear
- only the intended photographs are visible
- download and selection controls match the package
- the gallery works on a phone and computer

Do not treat a successful upload as a successful delivery. The final test must use the same route the client will use.

## Mistake 2: Using a vague or incorrect gallery name

Names such as "Final Photos," "Wedding Gallery" or "Edited Images" do not identify a specific assignment. They also make it easier to send the wrong link when several jobs are active.

The client-facing title should clearly identify the client or event. Your internal record should also connect the gallery to a unique job number and date.

For example:

- **Internal:** `NDY-2026-0148_Ada-Tunde_Wedding_2026-10-17`
- **Client-facing:** `Ada and Tunde's Wedding`

Review names, cover images and introductory messages for copied details from another client. Our guide to [naming and organising client galleries](/blog/how-to-name-and-organise-client-galleries/) provides a repeatable structure for galleries and files.

## Mistake 3: Uploading unfinished or duplicate photographs

Clients should not be the people who discover test exports, duplicates, inconsistent crops or unfinished retouching.

These errors often happen when the export folder also contains working files. A photograph is edited again, but both versions remain in the delivery folder. A low-resolution preview is mixed with full-size images. A black-and-white version appears without its colour companion.

Create a clean export folder for the final gallery. Review the exported files rather than relying only on the editing catalogue. Look for:

- accidental duplicates
- missing moments or sections
- inconsistent skin tones and exposure
- unfinished retouching
- incorrect crops or orientation
- test images and contact sheets
- mixed resolutions or colour spaces
- unexpected filename changes

Then review the collection in gallery order. Sequencing often reveals problems that are hard to notice when files are checked one by one.

## Mistake 4: Organising the gallery for the photographer, not the client

Internal folders may follow cameras, memory cards or editing batches. Those categories rarely help a client understand the finished assignment.

A wedding client is more likely to recognise "Getting Ready," "Ceremony," "Portraits" and "Reception" than "Camera A," "Card 4" and "Second Shooter."

Build client-facing sections around the experience. Use a small number of clear labels and arrange them in a natural order. Avoid creating many tiny sections that make browsing harder.

The first image, cover image and final image also matter. A polished sequence should feel intentional rather than like a folder sorted only by filename.

## Mistake 5: Hiding important instructions in a long message

A delivery message can contain every necessary detail and still be difficult to use.

If the link, password and next action are buried in several paragraphs, the client may miss them. Long terms and technical explanations should not stand between the client and the photographs.

Use a short structure:

1. Confirm that the gallery is ready.
2. Provide the link and access code.
3. Explain what is included.
4. State the next action.
5. Give the deadline or expiry date.
6. Provide one support channel.

Put the most important information near the top. If fuller usage or privacy terms are required, link to them separately.

## Mistake 6: Assuming the client understands file sizes

Terms such as "high resolution," "web size" and "optimised" may be familiar to photographers but not to every client.

Explain each option by purpose:

- **High resolution:** printing, archiving and large displays
- **Web size:** WhatsApp, email, websites and social media

If the client receives both versions, keep them in clearly separated download folders or use consistent filename suffixes.

Test a real download from the gallery. Confirm that the file has the expected dimensions, quality and filename. The guide to [sending photos without losing quality](/blog/best-way-to-send-photos-to-clients-without-losing-quality/) covers export and transfer checks in detail.

## Mistake 7: Leaving selection instructions unclear

A proofing gallery needs more guidance than "Please choose your favourites."

The client should know:

- how many photographs are included
- how to mark a favourite
- whether several people can select
- whether choices can be changed after submission
- what happens if the client wants extra images
- the selection deadline
- when retouching will be completed

Do not assume the gallery interface reflects the package. The software may allow unlimited favourites when the client bought ten retouched images.

Keep filenames stable after the gallery is sent. If a client refers to image `0047`, that identifier should still point to the same photograph in your workflow.

## Mistake 8: Collecting feedback across several channels

One client may tap favourites in the gallery, send screenshots on WhatsApp and email a separate list. If the studio treats every channel as final, selections and editing notes can conflict.

Choose one official selection method and say so in the delivery message. If a client sends a note elsewhere, transfer it to the approved record and confirm the consolidated instructions.

NDYLens supports client galleries where clients can select photographs and leave editing notes, with those choices connected to the client and booking workflow. This helps keep the final selection associated with the correct job instead of scattered across message threads.

## Mistake 9: Applying the wrong privacy settings

One privacy setting does not suit every assignment.

A public event gallery, private maternity session and confidential corporate shoot may require very different controls. Check whether the gallery should use:

- an unlisted link
- password protection
- access limited to named people
- disabled downloads
- separate private sections
- an expiry date

Do not describe a link as completely secure without evidence. A recipient may forward access or save a file. Explain practical limitations and avoid using private client photographs for marketing without appropriate permission.

Also confirm that gallery pages intended to be private are not discoverable through search engines or public navigation.

## Mistake 10: Making mobile viewing an afterthought

Many clients will first open a gallery from WhatsApp or email on a phone. A collection that works only on a large screen is not ready.

Test the page on a real phone. Check that images load at a practical speed, controls are easy to tap and the layout does not overflow the screen. Try mobile data as well as studio Wi-Fi.

Large original files may remain available for download, but the browsing experience should use suitable previews. Clients should not need to load every full-resolution file merely to review the gallery.

## Mistake 11: Forgetting the agreed deliverables

A beautiful gallery can still be incomplete.

Compare the delivery against the current quotation, package and any approved changes. Verify:

- the agreed number of edited photographs
- promised parts of the event or session
- web-size and high-resolution versions
- black-and-white copies, if included
- purchased extra images
- album, print or frame status
- any second delivery that remains outstanding

If part of the order will arrive later, state that clearly. The client should not have to guess whether a missing item is an oversight.

## Mistake 12: Giving no expiry or storage guidance

Clients need to know how long the gallery will remain available.

If it expires, provide the actual date rather than saying "available for 30 days." Send a reminder before closure, especially if the client has not downloaded the files or completed selections.

Do not position a gallery as the client's only permanent archive. Encourage the client to download and keep copies in more than one safe location. The studio should also follow a written retention and backup policy.

If restoring an expired gallery attracts a fee, disclose that before the expiry date.

## Mistake 13: Sending the gallery without a clear next step

The client should know whether the job is complete or waiting for another action.

The next step may be to:

- confirm that the gallery opens
- select favourites
- submit editing notes
- download the final files
- approve an album design
- choose print sizes
- confirm receipt of all deliverables

State one primary action and its deadline. Too many competing requests make it more likely that none will be completed.

Once the client responds, update the job status so the team knows whether it is awaiting selection, under retouching, delivered or archived.

## Mistake 14: Treating delivery as the end of communication

Sending a link is not the same as confirming a successful handoff.

Follow up if the client has not opened the gallery, submitted required selections or confirmed receipt. The purpose is not to pressure the client. It is to make sure a technical or communication problem has not blocked them.

After confirmation, explain any remaining product timelines and how the client can reach the studio later. A professional [gallery delivery workflow](/blog/how-to-deliver-a-photography-gallery-professionally/) includes preparation, communication, confirmation and closure.

## A final pre-delivery check

Use this checklist before sending a client gallery.

### Content

- Correct client, event and gallery title
- Agreed photographs and sections included
- No duplicates, tests or unfinished edits
- Consistent crops, colour and resolution
- Stable, useful filenames

### Access and privacy

- Client-facing link tested privately
- Password and permissions tested
- Download controls match the package
- Privacy settings suit the assignment
- Mobile layout and loading checked

### Communication

- Link and access details are easy to find
- Contents and file sizes are explained
- Selection process and deadline are clear
- Sharing and expiry guidance are stated
- Support channel and next action are included

### Record keeping

- Delivery date recorded
- Gallery status updated
- Selection and editing notes stored together
- Outstanding products recorded
- Client confirmation tracked

These checks support the broader standards in [what clients expect from a professional photo gallery](/blog/what-clients-expect-from-a-professional-photo-gallery/).

## Deliver the experience, not just the files

Photo gallery delivery is part of the photography service. The client should be able to open the link, understand the collection, make any required selections, download the correct files and know what happens next.

A short quality-control routine prevents most delivery mistakes. It also gives every client a consistent experience, regardless of who photographed, edited or sent the job.
