Your past clients are already in your phone. They enquired on WhatsApp, paid on WhatsApp and probably received their pictures there too. The trouble is that they’re now buried under hundreds of newer chats, and unless you reach out, the next time they need a photographer they may book whoever appears on their Instagram feed.

This guide shows you how to follow up with past photography clients on WhatsApp: when to message, what to say and how to keep it personal rather than pushy. You’ll also find ready-to-copy templates for the occasions that bring clients back most naturally.

## Why WhatsApp is the right place to follow up

For most photographers in Nigeria, WhatsApp is where the business actually happens. Enquiries, price lists, deposits, previews and final pictures often all pass through the same chat. That gives you a real advantage:

- **Clients already recognise you.** Your name and your previous conversation are right there.
- **It’s where they already are.** A WhatsApp message fits into their day; an email can easily sit unopened.
- **It feels personal.** A thoughtful message from the photographer who shot your wedding feels very different from a flyer on someone’s status.
- **Replying is easy.** A client can say “Yes, let’s book!” in seconds.

## Get your client list ready first

Before you send a single follow-up, you need to know who you’re messaging and why. Go through your chats and bookings from the past year or two and note down, for each client:

- Their name and WhatsApp number
- What you shot and when
- The next date that matters: a birthday, anniversary, due date or first birthday
- Anything personal worth remembering, like the baby’s name or the venue

If you use WhatsApp Business, add labels such as “Wedding client” or “Birthday client” so people are easier to find. Then put the key dates in a spreadsheet or calendar so they don’t depend on your memory. Our guide on [getting more repeat photography clients](/blog/how-to-get-more-repeat-photography-clients/) explains how to collect these details at booking, so your list stays complete from now on.

## When to follow up with past photography clients

A follow-up that arrives too late is just a nice message. One that arrives in time can become a booking. Use these timings as a starting point, then adjust them to how far ahead your clients usually plan.

- **After final delivery:** one to two weeks later, to check in
- **Birthdays:** three to four weeks before
- **Wedding anniversaries:** four to six weeks before
- **Maternity to newborn:** a few weeks before the due date
- **First birthdays:** six to eight weeks before, while parents are still planning
- **Christmas and New Year family shoots:** late October to early November
- **Christmas and New Year greetings:** on the day, with no sales pitch

Be gentle with pregnancy and newborn follow-ups. Keep the message warm and open, and never assume news you haven’t heard.

## WhatsApp messages for photographers: ready-to-copy templates

Replace everything in square brackets, and adjust the tone until it sounds like you.

### Check-in after delivery

> Hi [Name], I hope you’re still enjoying your pictures! I just wanted to check that everything downloaded properly and that you’re happy with how they turned out. It was a pleasure working with you.

### Upcoming birthday

> Hi [Name]! 🎂 It’s nearly time for [child’s name]’s birthday on [date]. Are you planning anything special this year? If you’d like pictures, I’d love to capture it again. Just let me know and I’ll send you available dates.

### Wedding anniversary

> Hi [Name] and [partner’s name]! Your first anniversary is coming up on [date], and we still smile when we look through your wedding pictures. Would you like to mark it with an anniversary shoot? It could be a relaxed session at home or somewhere that means something to you both.

### Maternity to newborn

> Hi [Name], I’ve been thinking of you as your due date gets closer, and I hope you’re feeling well. If you’d like newborn pictures, it helps to plan ahead so we can shoot in the baby’s first few weeks. Would you like me to hold some time for you?

### First birthday

> Hi [Name]! It’s hard to believe [baby’s name] will be one on [date]. If you’re planning a celebration or a cake smash, I’d love to be there. Shall I send you our birthday packages?

### Festive season family shoot

> Hi [Name], December is almost here! We’re opening dates for Christmas and New Year family shoots, and I wanted to give past clients first choice. Would you like me to save a date for your family?

### Holiday greeting (no pitch)

> Merry Christmas, [Name]! Thank you for letting us be part of your story this year. Wishing you and your family a joyful season.

### Reconnecting after a long silence

> Hi [Name], it’s [your name] from [studio name]. We photographed your [occasion] back in [month and year]. I was looking through our past work and remembered what a lovely day that was! If you have any plans for pictures this year, I’d be happy to help.

### Gentle reminder when there’s no reply

> Hi [Name], just bringing this up again in case it got buried. No pressure at all. Let me know if you’d like to book, and if not, I hope your [occasion] is wonderful!

## How to keep follow-ups personal, not spammy

- **Use their name and their occasion.** A message about their daughter’s birthday feels thoughtful. “Book your shoot now!!!” doesn’t.
- **Ask one clear question.** “Would you like to book?” or “Shall I send dates?” makes replying easy.
- **Keep it short.** Two or three sentences is plenty.
- **Send at sensible times.** Mid-morning or early evening is kinder than 11pm.
- **Attach a favourite picture from their shoot.** It brings the memory back instantly.
- **Save broadcasts for announcements.** Broadcast lists and status posts are fine for general news, but a follow-up should feel written for one person.
- **Respect a “no”.** If someone asks you to stop messaging, stop, and note it in your records.

## What to do when a client replies

**If they say yes:** reply quickly with available dates, your packages and how to secure the booking. Don’t let a warm reply go cold while you get round to it.

**If they say “not now”:** thank them, make a note and set a reminder for the next occasion.

> No problem at all, [Name]! Thank you for letting me know. I’ll be here whenever you’re ready.

**If they don’t reply:** send one gentle reminder after about a week. If there’s still no answer, leave it until the next date that matters.

**If they recommend someone instead:** thank them warmly and follow up with the person they mentioned. Our guide on [how to ask photography clients for referrals](/blog/how-to-ask-photography-clients-for-referrals/) covers how to encourage more of this.
