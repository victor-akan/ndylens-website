You’ve done the hard part. A client found you, trusted you with an important day, paid you and loved their pictures. Then the chat went quiet, and a year later someone else photographed their anniversary.

For most photographers in Nigeria, that isn’t a talent problem. It’s a tracking problem. Getting more repeat photography clients comes down to three things: knowing who you’ve worked with, knowing when they’re likely to need you again, and reaching out before they start scrolling Instagram for someone new. This guide walks you through each step, with WhatsApp messages you can copy and use today.

## Why repeat photography clients are worth the effort

A new client has to discover you, study your page, compare you with other photographers, ask for your prices and decide whether to trust you. A past client has already done all of that. They know your style, they know how you work and they’ve seen the finished pictures.

That makes a repeat booking easier in almost every way:

- **Less convincing.** You’re not selling yourself from scratch.
- **Smoother shoots.** They already know how you direct, how long editing takes and how delivery works.
- **Easier conversations about price.** They’ve seen what they get for your fee, so the discussion is about dates, not about whether you’re worth it.
- **More recommendations.** A client who books you twice is usually the one telling friends about you.

You don’t need a big marketing budget to win them back. You need a simple system.

## The next shoot is hidden in the last one

Every shoot you do points to a future one. Once you start seeing your bookings this way, your client list becomes a calendar of opportunities.

- **Weddings** can lead to anniversaries, maternity shoots, newborn sessions and family portraits.
- **Birthdays** come round every year, and milestones like a first birthday or a 40th are often celebrated in style.
- **Maternity shoots** lead naturally to newborn pictures, and then to a first birthday.
- **Family portraits** can become a yearly tradition, especially around Christmas and the New Year.

![A baby boy laughing at his first-birthday cake smash](/assets/blog/uploads/birthday-cake-smash.webp "A first birthday is rarely the last shoot a family needs")

For example, say you photographed Tolu and Kunle’s wedding in Abuja last November. That booking could become an anniversary shoot next November and, later, maternity and newborn sessions. None of it is guaranteed, but none of it will come to you if you don’t stay in touch.

## Step 1: Collect the dates that matter at booking

You can’t follow up on a date you never wrote down. The easiest time to collect it is while the client is booking, because they’re already sending you their details.

Alongside their name, number and shoot date, ask for:

- The date being celebrated (wedding date, birthday or due date)
- Children’s names and birthdays, for family or birthday shoots
- The partner’s name, for couples
- How they found you

You can build this into your booking confirmation:

> Hi [Name], thank you for booking with us! To help us prepare, please send the following:
>
> 1. Full name(s)
> 2. The date you’re celebrating (birthday, wedding date, due date, etc.)
> 3. Children’s names and birthdays, if they’ll be in the shoot
> 4. How you heard about us
>
> We can’t wait to work with you!

Most clients are happy to share this, and it shows you’re organised.

## Step 2: Make delivery something they remember

People rebook photographers they enjoyed working with, not just ones whose pictures they liked. What happens after the shoot matters more than many photographers realise.

- Tell clients when to expect their pictures, then deliver on or before that date.
- Make picture selection simple. Endless back-and-forth over which shots to edit wears everyone out.
- Send a short, personal thank-you when the final pictures go out.

> Hi [Name], your final pictures are ready! It was a real pleasure capturing your [occasion]. If anything needs a small adjustment, just let me know. Thank you for trusting us with such a special day.

## Step 3: Keep a proper client record

If your client history lives only inside WhatsApp chats, it will get buried. A few hundred conversations later, you won’t remember whose daughter turns two next month.

At minimum, keep a spreadsheet with a row for every client and columns for:

- Name and WhatsApp number
- Shoot type and shoot date
- The next key date (birthday, anniversary, due date)
- When to follow up
- Notes, such as a child’s name or a location they loved

For example: *Ngozi Eze · maternity shoot, March · baby due mid-May · follow up late April · wants an outdoor newborn session.*

If you use WhatsApp Business, labels such as “Wedding client” or “Birthday client” help you find people faster. Labels won’t remind you of dates, though, so the record still matters.

## Step 4: Follow up before the date, not on it

This is where most repeat bookings are won or lost. Message a client on the day of their anniversary and it’s too late: they’ve booked someone else or decided not to bother.

As a rough guide:

- **Birthdays:** three to four weeks before
- **Anniversaries:** four to six weeks before
- **Newborn shoots:** a few weeks before the due date
- **Christmas and New Year family shoots:** by early November, before December fills up

Here’s a birthday follow-up you can adapt:

> Hi [Name]! Can you believe [child’s name] turns [age] on [date]? We loved capturing last year’s celebration. Would you like to book a birthday shoot this year? I have a few dates open that week.

And one for an anniversary:

> Hi [Name]! It’s almost a year since we shot your wedding, and your anniversary is coming up on [date]. Would you like to book an anniversary shoot to celebrate?

## Step 5: Give past clients a reason to come back

A well-timed message is often enough, but a small extra can help someone decide sooner:

- **A returning-client perk:** a few extra edited pictures or a modest discount on their next session.
- **First pick of busy dates:** let past clients book the festive season before you announce it publicly.
- **Milestone bundles:** a maternity session paired with newborn pictures, for example.

> Hi [Name], as one of our past clients, you’ll get [perk] on your next shoot with us. Whether it’s a birthday, anniversary or family portrait, just send me a message when you’re ready and I’ll share available dates.

Don’t discount so heavily that repeat work stops being profitable. The aim is to make coming back feel easy and appreciated, not cheap.

## Step 6: Track who actually comes back

Once a quarter, look through your bookings and mark the ones that came from past clients. Then ask yourself:

- Which shoot types bring people back most often?
- Which follow-up messages got replies, and which were ignored?
- Who went quiet after their shoot, and was there a reason?

Simply counting returning-client shoots each quarter tells you whether your follow-ups are working.

## Common mistakes that cost you repeat bookings

- **Only messaging when business is slow.** Clients can tell. Reach out because a date is coming, not because your calendar is empty.
- **Sending the same flyer to everyone.** A generic promo is easy to ignore. Mention their name and their shoot.
- **Following up too late.** A week before a birthday, most parents have already made their plans.
- **Chasing too hard.** One polite reminder is fine if there’s no reply. After that, wait for the next occasion.
