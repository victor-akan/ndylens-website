NDYLens event reminders help photographers organise important client dates and follow up before the next photography opportunity has passed. Instead of relying on memory or scrolling through old WhatsApp chats, you can keep the client record, relevant date and follow-up plan together.

The aim is not to message every client about every occasion. A useful reminder tells you who may need photography again, why the date matters and when to review the relationship. You can then send the message yourself or use an appropriate automated WhatsApp follow-up.

This practical guide shows how to prepare client records, choose useful events, set sensible timing and follow up in a way that still feels personal.

## What an important client event means

An important event is a date or planning window that may create a genuine photography need. The date should have a clear connection to the work you provide.

Common examples include:

- a birthday for a portrait, family or cake-smash client
- a wedding or engagement anniversary
- a child's birthday or dedication anniversary
- a recurring company conference, awards night or end-of-year event
- an annual school, church or community programme
- a maternity, newborn or family milestone that the client willingly shared
- a seasonal portrait period that suits the client's previous booking

The event does not have to be the date of the next shoot. It can be the date that helps you work backwards to a useful follow-up window. A couple may want anniversary photographs before the anniversary itself. A company may need several weeks to approve an event photographer. A family may plan a birthday shoot early enough to prepare outfits, a location and prints.

Start with dates that already exist in a legitimate client relationship. Do not collect personal information simply because it might become useful later.

## Why the follow-up needs to happen before the event

A message sent on the event date can still be thoughtful, but it may be too late to win the booking. By then, the client may have completed the shoot, chosen another photographer or decided not to organise one.

The right lead time depends on the service:

| Event or service | When to begin reviewing the client | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple portrait or birthday session | Two to four weeks before | Gives the client time to choose a date and prepare |
| Anniversary session | Three to six weeks before | Allows for styling, location and couple availability |
| Family or milestone session | Three to six weeks before | Gives several people time to coordinate |
| Corporate or recurring event | Six to twelve weeks before | Allows for approval, quotation and logistics |
| Seasonal campaign | Before the studio's booking window opens | Lets the studio contact suitable clients before slots fill |

These are starting points, not fixed rules. Use your actual booking process, production time and availability. The guide on [how early to remind clients to book a photoshoot](/blog/how-early-should-you-remind-clients-to-book-a-photoshoot/) explains how to choose a lead time by service.

## Prepare the client record before creating a reminder

The reminder can only be as useful as the information behind it. Before setting one, review the client record and confirm the details that affect the follow-up.

### Confirm the client and contact details

Check the client's name, phone number and any communication preference you have recorded. A wrong number or duplicate record can turn a helpful reminder into an awkward message.

### Connect the date to a real booking

Record why the date matters. "Anniversary" is more useful when it is connected to the couple's wedding booking and date. "Annual conference" is more useful when it points to the company, previous event and service delivered.

NDYLens can keep client details and booking history together. That context helps you see what happened before you contact the client again.

### Check the exact date and certainty

Separate confirmed dates from estimates. If the client said an event is usually held in November but did not give a date, record that as a planning window rather than inventing a precise day.

### Add only useful context

Useful notes may include the previous service, preferred type of shoot, location, planning contact or a relevant request from the last booking. Avoid unnecessary personal details. The record should help you serve the client, not become a private biography.

### Decide who owns the follow-up

If several people work in the studio, make one person responsible for reviewing the reminder. A reminder with no owner can remain untouched even when it appears at the right time.

## How to use NDYLens event reminders step by step

The exact workflow should match the way your studio handles bookings, but the following sequence keeps the process clear.

### 1. Add or review the client

Start with the correct client record. Confirm the contact details, previous booking and event information before you create a new reminder.

If you are organising many past clients, work through them in small groups. Begin with recent clients whose records are easier to verify. The guide to [remembering every client's important dates](/blog/how-to-remember-every-clients-important-dates/) provides a practical structure for cleaning and recording the information.

### 2. Record the event and why it matters

Choose a clear event type, such as birthday, wedding anniversary or annual company event. Add the confirmed date or planning window and connect it to the relevant client or booking.

The reason matters because it shapes the message. A birthday follow-up should not sound like a corporate event reminder. An anniversary message should recognise the couple and the previous work without pretending you know more about their plans than you do.

### 3. Choose the review date

Work backwards from the event. Allow enough time for the client to decide, for your studio to check availability and for both sides to prepare.

The review date is when the photographer should assess the record. It does not always have to be the moment a message is sent. You may need to check availability, review the previous gallery or confirm the event date first.

### 4. Choose manual or automatic follow-up

NDYLens lets a studio keep track of important dates and know when it is time to follow up. You can use the reminder to send the message personally or set up an automated WhatsApp message when the situation is routine and the details are dependable.

Use a manual review when:

- the event date is uncertain
- the previous booking had a complaint or unresolved issue
- the service or price needs a custom decision
- the client has already replied through another channel
- the occasion is sensitive
- you need to confirm availability before making an offer

Automation is better suited to stable, approved follow-ups with accurate details and a clear stopping rule.

### 5. Prepare a relevant message

A good follow-up gives the client a reason to understand why you are reaching out. It should identify the occasion, connect naturally to the previous relationship and make the next step easy.

For example:

> Hi Ada, I hope you are well. Your wedding anniversary is coming up next month, and I wanted to check whether you would like to plan an anniversary session this year. I can share available dates if you are interested.

The message is specific without assuming the client wants to book. It invites a reply instead of forcing a sales conversation.

### 6. Review before sending

Check the name, date, event, service and timing. Confirm that the client has not opted out and that another team member has not already contacted them.

If the message is automatic, test the approved template with a small group of clean records before using it more widely.

### 7. Record the outcome

The reminder is not complete when the message leaves the system. Record what happened next:

- the client replied and requested dates
- a new enquiry or booking was created
- the client declined
- the date was corrected
- there was no response
- the client asked not to receive future follow-ups
- the reminder should return next year

This outcome keeps the record useful for the next person who reviews it.

## Three example event-reminder workflows

### Birthday portrait client

A family booked a birthday portrait session last year. The client's record contains the birthday date and the service delivered.

Set a review several weeks before the next birthday. When the reminder appears, confirm the date, review the previous booking and check available portrait slots. Send a short message that refers to the upcoming birthday and offers the next step.

If the family books, create the new booking and close the follow-up. If they are not interested, record the response instead of continuing the same sequence.

### Wedding anniversary client

A couple's wedding date creates a recurring anniversary. The photographer may review the record four to six weeks before that date, depending on the studio's planning time.

The follow-up can mention the anniversary and ask whether the couple would like an anniversary session. Avoid claiming that every couple celebrates with photographs. The reminder creates a timely opportunity, not a guaranteed sale.

### Annual corporate event

A company hired the studio for an awards night. The following year's event may require a quotation, internal approval and several planning contacts.

Set the review earlier than you would for a portrait client. Check the previous scope, decision-maker and event period. Contact the company while its planning is still open, and be prepared to confirm availability and provide a current quotation.

## Write follow-ups that still feel personal

Automation should protect timing, not remove judgement. Before approving a message, ask five questions:

1. **Is the reason clear?** The client should understand why the studio is contacting them.
2. **Is the date correct?** Do not send a confident message from an uncertain record.
3. **Does the wording fit the relationship?** A recent wedding client and a corporate organiser need different language.
4. **Is the next step easy?** Ask for a reply, offer available dates or point to a clear booking step.
5. **Will the message stop when it should?** A reply, booking, decline, correction or opt-out should close or pause the sequence.

Keep the message concise. Do not combine congratulations, a discount, a referral request, a review request and a booking pitch in one follow-up.

## When not to send the reminder

Pause or remove a client-facing follow-up when:

- the client asked not to be contacted
- the event information is wrong or unconfirmed
- the studio cannot offer the service or has no suitable availability
- there is an unresolved complaint, refund or payment dispute
- the occasion may be sensitive and requires human review
- the client already booked or replied elsewhere
- another team member has already handled the conversation

You can still keep an internal task that asks a team member to review the situation. The reminder does not have to trigger a message.

## Build a simple event-reminder routine

Event reminders work best when the studio reviews them consistently.

### Every week

- check reminders due in the next seven to fourteen days
- confirm who owns each action
- review automatic messages waiting to send
- update outcomes from recent conversations
- correct dates or contact details discovered during follow-up

### Every month

- look further ahead for corporate and seasonal work
- review reminders that produced no response
- remove duplicates and outdated records
- check whether the lead time was too early or too late
- prepare capacity before contacting a larger client group

### Every quarter

- review which reminder types created useful conversations
- compare follow-ups with repeat enquiries and bookings
- identify messages that clients ignored or misunderstood
- update templates, lead times and stopping rules

Measure your own process. Do not assume a fixed increase in bookings. A reminder may help because the timing was right, the previous client experience was strong and the service matched the client's need.

## Keep client information accurate and appropriate

Use client information only for legitimate studio work and the communication the client would reasonably expect. Keep contact details and dates accurate. Respect opt-outs and communication preferences.

If a client corrects an anniversary or event date, update the record before the next reminder. If the information should no longer be used, remove it or stop the follow-up.

Accurate information protects the relationship. It also makes automation safer because the system is not acting on details that a person already knows are wrong.
