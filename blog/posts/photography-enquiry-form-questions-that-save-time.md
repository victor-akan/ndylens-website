A good photography enquiry form should save time for both you and the potential client. It should collect enough information for you to understand the request, check whether the job is a fit and reply with a useful next step. It should not feel like a long application for the privilege of seeing your price list.

The challenge is deciding which questions belong on the form. Ask too little and you will still need several messages before you can help. Ask too much and interested clients may abandon the form or provide rushed answers that are not useful.

The best photography enquiry form questions are brief, relevant and connected to a decision. This guide gives you a practical core form, optional questions for different photography services and a simple way to turn every completed form into an organised booking conversation.

## What should a photography enquiry form achieve?

An enquiry form is the beginning of a conversation, not a complete client questionnaire. Its job is to help you answer five questions:

1. Who is contacting you?
2. What photography service do they need?
3. When and where will it happen?
4. Is the request clear enough for you to recommend a package or next step?
5. How and when should you respond?

Information that does not help with one of those decisions may belong later in your workflow. Detailed family group lists, wedding timelines, shot lists, editing preferences and delivery instructions are usually more appropriate after the client has chosen a service or confirmed a booking.

Keeping that boundary clear makes the form easier to complete and the answers easier to use.

## The essential photography enquiry form questions

Most photographers can begin with the following fields and adapt them to their services.

### 1. What is your name?

Ask for the person's full name or first and last name. This gives you a professional way to address the reply and helps prevent duplicate records.

If someone is enquiring on behalf of a company, couple or family, you can collect the organisation or partner's name separately when it is relevant.

### 2. What is the best email address and phone number to reach you?

Collect at least one dependable contact method. If your studio commonly responds on WhatsApp, label the phone field clearly as a WhatsApp number and ask the client to include the country code when necessary.

Do not ask for the same contact detail twice in different sections. Explain which channel you will use for your first reply so the client knows where to look.

### 3. Which photography service are you interested in?

Use a short list based on the services you actually provide, such as:

- Wedding or traditional event photography
- Birthday or event coverage
- Portrait session
- Maternity, newborn or family photography
- Corporate event or headshots
- Product or commercial photography
- Other

An “Other” option with a short text box helps people describe a legitimate request that does not fit the list. Avoid displaying services you no longer offer simply to make the form appear comprehensive.

### 4. What is the event or session date?

The date helps you check availability and understand urgency. Give the client a way to say that the date is flexible or not yet confirmed.

A required date field without a flexible option can block early-stage enquiries. Someone planning a brand shoot may be choosing between several dates, while a couple may know the month but not have secured a venue.

### 5. Where will the photography take place?

Ask for the city and state at minimum. Request a full venue or address only when you need it at the enquiry stage.

Location affects availability, travel, timing and the type of coverage you can recommend. A simple “Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt or another city” answer is often enough for the first response.

### 6. Tell us briefly what you are planning

Use one open text field for the client to explain the request in their own words. A useful prompt is:

> Please tell us what you are planning, the kind of coverage you need and anything important you would like us to know.

Keep the prompt focused. “Tell us everything about your dream shoot” may sound friendly, but it often produces long answers without the specific facts you need.

### 7. How many people, products or coverage hours are involved?

The wording should match the selected service. A wedding photographer may ask for expected coverage hours and the number of events. A portrait studio may ask how many people will be photographed. A commercial photographer may need the number of products, final images or locations.

If your form cannot show different questions based on the selected service, use one optional scope field with examples rather than displaying every service-specific question to everyone.

### 8. How did you hear about us?

This question helps you understand whether the enquiry came from a referral, Instagram, Google, an event vendor, a past client or another source.

Keep the answer list short and include “Other.” When the client selects “Referral,” an optional “Who can we thank?” field can help you recognise the person who recommended you.

### 9. What is the best time or way to contact you?

This can be useful when your next step is a phone call or WhatsApp conversation. Offer realistic options, such as email, WhatsApp message or phone call, and do not promise a channel that your studio does not monitor consistently.

If you normally respond by message first, you may not need this question. Every field should earn its place.

## A short enquiry form template

Here is a practical version you can adapt:

1. Full name
2. Email address
3. WhatsApp number
4. Photography service required
5. Event or session date, with a “not confirmed” option
6. City or location
7. Brief description of the request
8. Number of people, products, locations or expected coverage hours
9. How did you hear about us?
10. Preferred contact method

That is enough for many studios. If a question does not affect availability, fit, pricing or the next step, consider collecting it later.

## Service-specific questions to add only when relevant

Conditional questions make a form feel shorter because clients see only what applies to their request.

### Wedding and traditional event enquiries

Useful optional questions include:

- Which events need coverage?
- Are the ceremony and reception on the same date?
- Approximately how many hours of coverage do you expect?
- Has the venue been confirmed?
- Do you need both photography and video?

Do not request the complete wedding timeline at this stage. First determine whether you are available and which coverage options are relevant.

### Portrait, birthday and family sessions

Consider asking:

- How many people will be photographed?
- Is this a studio, home or outdoor session?
- What is the occasion?
- Is the date fixed or flexible?
- Are there any access or mobility needs the studio should plan for?

Avoid asking clients to select technical lighting or editing styles unless those choices are part of clearly explained packages.

### Corporate headshots and events

Useful questions may include:

- What organisation is making the enquiry?
- How many people need headshots, or how long is the event?
- Will everyone be photographed at one location?
- What are the required outputs and delivery deadline?
- Who will approve the proposal?

These details help you decide whether to send a standard package or prepare a custom quotation.

### Product and commercial photography

Ask only for the scope needed to plan an initial discussion:

- What products, services or campaign will be photographed?
- Approximately how many products or final images are required?
- Where will the images be used?
- Is styling, talent or location production required?
- What is the preferred shoot and delivery date?

Commercial enquiries often need a follow-up call or brief. The form should identify that need, not attempt to replace the full production brief.

## Should you ask for the client's budget?

A budget field can help when you create custom scopes, but it is not essential for every enquiry form.

Ask for a budget range when it will change the production approach, deliverables or recommendation. Explain its purpose in the field label, for example: “Do you have an approved budget range? This helps us recommend a suitable scope.”

For fixed packages, it may be clearer to display a starting price or send the relevant package after checking the request. A mandatory budget question with no context can feel like a test of whether the client deserves a reply.

Use ranges rather than requiring an exact figure, and include “Not sure yet” when that is a valid stage in your sales process.

## Questions to leave out of the first form

Remove questions that create effort without improving your first decision. Common examples include:

- A full event timeline before availability is confirmed
- A complete shot list
- Detailed editing instructions
- Family group combinations
- Login details for social media or delivery platforms
- Sensitive identification information that is not necessary
- Several versions of “Tell us about yourself”
- Questions answered clearly on the page immediately above the form

Also avoid collecting personal information simply because another photographer's form includes it. Know why you need each field, limit access to responses and keep the information only as long as it serves a legitimate business purpose.

## How to make the form easier to complete

### Keep required fields to the minimum

Name, contact details, service, date, location and a short description are often the core. Mark optional fields clearly so the client does not have to guess.

### Use plain language

Ask “Where will the photos be used?” instead of “State the licensing scope.” Ask “How many final images do you expect?” instead of “Specify asset volume.” You can introduce technical terms later when you explain them.

### Show the expected response time

Tell clients when they should hear from you. Use a promise you can keep, such as “We reply within one business day,” only if that reflects your actual workflow.

### Make the form work on a phone

Many clients will open your link from Instagram or WhatsApp. Use large fields, sensible answer choices and as little typing as possible. Test the entire form on a real phone, including the confirmation screen.

### Confirm that the form was submitted

After submission, show a clear success message and explain the next step. If possible, send a confirmation by email or your chosen channel. Never leave the client wondering whether the button worked.

## What to do after an enquiry form is submitted

A form saves time only when it connects to a dependable process.

Review the response and place it into one of four states:

1. **Ready for a response:** You can confirm availability and send the relevant package or next step.
2. **Needs clarification:** One important detail is missing or unclear.
3. **Needs internal review:** You must check travel, schedule, team capacity or a custom requirement.
4. **Not a fit:** The service, date, location or scope does not match your studio.

## Common enquiry form mistakes

### Making every question compulsory

Clients may not know the venue, exact hours or final deliverables yet. Require only the information you truly need to respond.

### Asking one generic open question

“How can we help?” is easy to publish but often creates extra messages. Pair an open description with simple fields for service, date and location.

### Using a form as a barrier to pricing

The form should guide the client to the right information. It should not hide every indication of price behind a long questionnaire.

### Failing to test notifications

Submit the form yourself from a phone and another email address. Check that the response arrives, the client sees confirmation and all required fields are included.

### Collecting responses without a follow-up system

A perfect form cannot rescue a slow or forgotten reply. Decide who checks submissions, how quickly they respond and where the next action is recorded.

## Final photography enquiry form checklist

Before publishing or updating your form, confirm that:

- Every required question affects your first response
- The service list matches what you currently offer
- Clients can indicate that a date or venue is not confirmed
- Service-specific questions appear only when relevant
- Labels are clear on a mobile screen
- The form explains what happens after submission
- Notifications reach the correct person
- A completed enquiry has an owner, next action and follow-up date
- You tested the form from submission to reply

A useful form does not collect the most information. It collects the right information at the right stage. Start with the short core, add only the service-specific questions that change your decision and make the next step obvious.
