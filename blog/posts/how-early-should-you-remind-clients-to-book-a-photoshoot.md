A client may genuinely want another photoshoot and still fail to book. The birthday feels far away, the family has not chosen outfits, or the company has not approved its event plan. Then the date gets close, the client starts looking for a photographer and your calendar is already full.

The answer is not to remind every client months in advance. It is to contact each person early enough to make a decision, but close enough that the need feels real.

Good **photoshoot booking reminder timing** starts with the date the client needs the photographs, then works backwards through preparation, decision time and your own availability. The right lead time changes with the service, season and relationship.

## The short answer

Use these ranges as starting points, then adjust them with your own booking history:

- **Headshots and simple portraits:** start 1 to 3 weeks before the preferred session date.
- **Family, maternity and milestone sessions:** start 3 to 6 weeks before the ideal session window.
- **Birthdays and small celebrations:** start 4 to 8 weeks before the event.
- **Corporate sessions and recurring company events:** start 6 to 12 weeks before the likely date.
- **Weddings and other high-planning events:** start several months ahead, based on how early your clients normally secure photographers.
- **Peak periods:** add more time when your calendar usually fills faster, such as December celebrations, school holidays or popular wedding weekends.

These are planning ranges, not promises. A photographer whose portrait calendar fills in three days needs a different reminder window from a studio that schedules six weeks ahead. Review your own enquiries, confirmed bookings and declined dates before settling on a rule.

## Work backwards from the real deadline

The event date is not always the deadline.

If a client needs framed photographs for a birthday, the useful deadline may be the date the finished prints must be ready. If a company needs headshots for a website launch, the deadline includes editing, selection and delivery. If a family wants an outdoor session, the plan may need room for weather changes.

Build the reminder date from five parts:

1. **Delivery time:** How long do selection, editing, review and delivery take?
2. **Session preparation:** Does the client need outfits, a location, makeup, permits or several participants?
3. **Decision time:** How long does this client or organisation usually take to approve a booking?
4. **Calendar pressure:** How quickly does your schedule fill for this service and season?
5. **A small buffer:** What happens if the first date is unavailable or one step is delayed?

Suppose a family wants photographs ready for a child's birthday. If editing and delivery take one week, the family needs another week to prepare, and your preferred dates usually fill two weeks ahead, contacting them only seven days before the birthday is too late. The correct reminder should arrive before those periods begin to overlap.

## Choose the window by photoshoot type

Different sessions create different decisions. Use the service as the first filter, then adapt the timing to the client.

### Headshots and simple portrait sessions

A straightforward studio portrait may need less planning than an event. A reminder 1 to 3 weeks before the preferred date can give the client time to choose an outfit and confirm availability without making the session feel distant.

Contact earlier when several employees are involved, the location is outside your studio, or the client needs photographs for a fixed launch date.

### Family and milestone sessions

Family sessions often require several people to agree on a date. Parents may need to plan outfits, hair appointments, transport and a suitable time for children.

A 3 to 6 week starting window gives the family room to coordinate. For maternity sessions, plan around the client's preferred stage and comfort rather than treating the due date as an ordinary event date. Keep the message helpful and allow the client to choose what works.

### Birthdays and small celebrations

For birthday portraits, child dedications and similar celebrations, 4 to 8 weeks can be a useful starting range. Contacting early helps the client compare the photoshoot with other event tasks and gives you room to offer available dates.

If the client wants photographs before the celebration, remind them based on the delivery date, not only the birthday itself.

### Weddings and large events

Weddings involve venues, vendors, schedules and budgets. A useful reminder may need to arrive several months before the event, especially when the date falls in a busy period.

Do not rely on one universal wedding lead time. Look at your own records: how far ahead did confirmed couples first enquire, and when did you begin declining dates because the calendar was full? Use that evidence to set the first reminder.

### Corporate and recurring events

Corporate decisions may move through several people. A 6 to 12 week starting window can give the contact time to confirm the date, request a quotation and obtain approval.

For annual conferences, awards, retreats or end-of-year events, an earlier internal reminder may be useful. Review last year's booking, identify the likely organiser and check whether the event is returning before sending a sales message.

## Adjust for the season

The same client may need a different reminder in March and December.

Move the first reminder earlier when:

- your calendar normally fills faster
- clients need weekend dates
- travel or venue access requires more planning
- the session involves several households or departments
- the client needs printed products by a fixed date
- public holidays reduce working days
- outdoor conditions may require an alternative date

In Nigeria, December can combine weddings, family reunions, corporate events and holiday sessions. A message that would be early in a quieter month may be appropriate before that period. The decision should come from your real capacity, not from sending more messages simply because the season is busy.

## Use two reminders instead of one late push

A simple sequence is often more useful than one urgent message.

### First reminder: planning stage

Send the first message while the client still has choices. Mention the relevant occasion, ask whether a session is being considered and offer to share current availability.

> Hi [Name], [occasion] is coming up on [date]. If you are planning photographs this year, I can share the available session dates and current options. Would that be useful?

This message should start a conversation, not imply that a booking already exists.

### Second reminder: decision stage

If the client has not replied, one shorter follow-up can arrive closer to your booking cutoff.

> Hi [Name], I am checking once more about photographs for [occasion]. We are now planning the remaining dates around [period]. If you would like the available options, reply here and I will send them. If your plans have changed, no problem.

Stop after the client books, declines, asks not to be contacted or the planning window closes. Do not turn a useful reminder into repeated pressure.

## Do not confuse a reminder with a booking confirmation

A past client's birthday or anniversary does not reserve a date. A reply such as “I am interested” is not necessarily a confirmed booking either.

Keep these stages separate:

- **Internal reminder:** your studio should review the client and occasion.
- **Booking invitation:** the client is asked whether they want current options.
- **Active enquiry:** the client is discussing a date, service or price.
- **Confirmed booking:** the client has completed your stated booking requirement.
- **Session reminder:** the confirmed client receives the final date, time, location and preparation details.

Clear stages prevent two common mistakes: promising availability that no longer exists and sending booking reminders to someone who has not booked.

## Let the client's behaviour change the timing

Your first rule can come from the service, but the final decision should include the client record.

Contact earlier when the client:

- usually takes time to choose a date
- coordinates several people
- needs internal approval
- travels for sessions
- orders prints or albums for a deadline
- previously asked you to remind them early

Contact later, send a personal message or skip the reminder when the client:

- recently declined the same offer
- has an unresolved complaint or delivery issue
- asked not to receive follow-ups
- is dealing with a sensitive personal circumstance
- has already booked another service with you
- has outdated or uncertain event information

The purpose is not to maximise the number of messages. It is to make each contact relevant.

## Find your best reminder timing from studio records

You do not need a complicated report. Review recent bookings for each major service and record:

- event or required-delivery date
- date of first enquiry
- date the booking was confirmed
- date you first contacted the client, if it was a repeat booking
- whether the client said the message was early, late or well timed
- whether your preferred dates were already full
- the final outcome

Look for a practical pattern. If most family clients need several weeks to coordinate, move that reminder earlier. If portrait clients repeatedly ignore messages sent two months ahead but reply closer to the date, shorten the window. If you lose December work because you reach out after weekends are full, schedule the internal review earlier.

Keep the event date and reminder date as separate fields. The guide to [remembering every client's important dates](/blog/how-to-remember-every-clients-important-dates/) explains how to build a reliable date record without depending on old chats.

## A weekly timing review

Set aside a short weekly review for upcoming client dates and booking windows.

For each record, check:

1. Is the event date confirmed and still relevant?
2. What service would the client realistically need?
3. What is the latest comfortable booking date for that service?
4. Has the client already replied, booked or opted out?
5. Does the message need a person to review it first?
6. Who owns the next action?

This review keeps reminders tied to current availability. It also catches cases where the event remains months away but the useful booking window is already opening.

## Start with three timing rules

Do not build a different schedule for every possible photoshoot on the first day.

Start with three services you book often. For each one, write down:

- the first reminder window
- the point when availability becomes limited
- the latest comfortable booking date
- the preparation and delivery time
- the follow-up limit
- the stopping rule

Test those rules for a month. Compare the reminder dates with actual replies and confirmed bookings, then adjust them.

The best time to remind a client is early enough for a calm decision and late enough for the need to feel real. Work backwards from the real deadline, allow for preparation and delivery, then use your own booking history to refine the timing.
