Gallery delivery can look like the final step in a photography job, but it usually contains several smaller steps. The photographer prepares a gallery, sends access to the client, waits for selections, receives editing notes, completes the work and delivers the finished photographs.

Each step is manageable on its own. The difficulty begins when the booking details live in one place, selections arrive as screenshots, editing notes are scattered across messages and the final delivery is disconnected from the client record.

**NDYLens gallery delivery** is designed to keep those parts connected. Photographers can share client galleries, receive photo selections and editing notes, and move the work towards delivery within the same client and booking workflow.

This practical guide explains what that connection changes, what the photographer still needs to verify and how a studio can use NDYLens without losing control of its creative process.

## Why gallery delivery becomes difficult to manage

A gallery is not only a collection of photographs. It is also a working point between the studio and the client.

Depending on the job, the client may need to:

- review proof images
- choose a set number of favourites
- identify photographs for retouching
- leave editing instructions
- confirm that the final work is complete
- receive the finished photographs

When these actions happen in different places, the team has to reconstruct the process manually. One person may check the gallery while another searches WhatsApp for the final selection. A note about removing an object may sit beside an old screenshot rather than the selected image. The photographer may finish the files but forget to update the booking.

The main problem is not the number of tools. It is the missing link between the client, the booking, the gallery and the next action.

## Start from the client and booking context

NDYLens treats gallery work as part of the client journey rather than an isolated upload.

The client record and booking provide the context for the photographs. They help the studio identify who the work belongs to, which job is active and where the gallery fits in the wider process.

That connection matters when a studio is handling several sessions at once. A birthday shoot, wedding and corporate event may all be waiting for client action, but each has a different scope, delivery promise and next step.

Keeping the gallery associated with the correct client and booking reduces the chance of working from a generic folder or an incomplete message thread. It also gives team members a shared reference when they need to check the job.

## Share a gallery as part of the workflow

NDYLens supports galleries that photographers can share with clients. This gives the client a defined place to review photographs instead of relying on a long sequence of compressed previews or numbered screenshots in chat.

A gallery can make the review stage easier to understand because the photographs remain together. The photographer can direct the client to one collection, while the client can review the work in the context intended by the studio.

The photographer still needs to prepare the gallery carefully. Before sharing it, confirm that:

- the correct client and job are attached
- the intended photographs are present
- filenames and image order are useful
- unfinished or duplicate images are removed
- the package and required client action are clear

NDYLens provides the connected gallery process. It does not replace the studio's quality control or the promises made in the booking.

## Keep photo selections connected to the job

Selections often create the most avoidable confusion in a photography workflow.

A client may send ten screenshots on WhatsApp, add three filenames by email and later change two choices in another message. If the studio does not establish one official selection route, the final list becomes difficult to trust.

NDYLens allows clients to select photographs in the gallery. Those choices remain connected to the client and booking workflow, so the studio has a clearer record of the images the client chose.

This helps the photographer answer practical questions:

- Which photographs were selected?
- Do the choices belong to the correct booking?
- Is the client action complete?
- What work should happen next?

The studio should still explain the selection rules. Tell the client how many photographs to choose, when the selection is due and whether choices can be changed after submission. Software cannot correct an unclear package or an unstated deadline.

## Attach editing notes to the selection process

An image number without its editing instruction is only half a record.

Clients may want a crop adjusted, a background distraction removed or a particular finishing preference considered. When these notes arrive separately from the selection, the editor has to match several conversations to the correct photograph.

NDYLens supports editing notes alongside the gallery selection process. This keeps the client's instructions closer to the photographs and booking they relate to.

The benefit is not that every request should be accepted automatically. The photographer still decides what is technically appropriate, what is included in the package and whether a request changes the agreed scope.

A clear workflow should therefore include three actions:

1. Record the client's note with the relevant selection.
2. Review the request against the package and creative standard.
3. Clarify anything ambiguous before final editing begins.

That record gives the editor better context and reduces the risk of working from an outdated chat message.

## Move from selection to delivery with fewer handoffs

The transition between selection and final delivery is where small workflow gaps become expensive.

If the client has completed their choices but the studio does not notice, editing can be delayed. If the photographer finishes the work but the booking still appears to be waiting for selection, another team member may follow up unnecessarily.

Keeping gallery activity within the client and booking workflow helps the studio understand what stage the work has reached. The process can move from gallery review to selection, editing and delivery without creating a separate record for each stage.

This does not mean the creative work happens automatically. The photographer or editor still needs to:

- review the selected files
- complete the promised editing
- export the correct versions
- check the final photographs
- confirm that delivery matches the package

NDYLens simplifies the administrative connection around that work. It helps the studio keep the client action and delivery status attached to the job.

## Deliver photographs through the client journey

NDYLens supports delivering photographs through WhatsApp after the gallery and selection process. This fits the communication habits of many Nigerian photography clients, who already use WhatsApp to discuss bookings and receive service updates.

The delivery message should remain clear and practical. It should tell the client that the photographs are ready, identify the job and explain any immediate next step.

Before delivery, the studio should test the exact files or link the client will receive. Confirm that the work opens correctly, the correct versions are present and the delivery matches the agreed package.

For a complete preparation routine, use the [client gallery delivery checklist](/blog/a-client-gallery-delivery-checklist-for-photographers/) before sending anything.

WhatsApp is the communication route, not a substitute for file-quality checks, backup or appropriate access decisions. The photographer remains responsible for the quality, retention and privacy practices applied to the work.

## Preserve useful history after delivery

Delivery should close the current job without erasing the relationship.

When gallery activity is connected to the client record, the studio retains useful history about the booking. The team can see that the client moved through selection and delivery rather than treating the photographs as an unrelated link sent months ago.

This history can help when the client returns. A photographer can understand the previous service and booking context without rebuilding the story from old messages.

NDYLens also connects client management with follow-up. That means delivery can become part of a longer relationship that includes future service reminders and relevant client dates.

The aim is not to turn every completed gallery into an immediate sales message. A delivery follow-up should first confirm that the client received the work successfully. Future outreach should be relevant to the real relationship and the studio's communication choices.

## A simple NDYLens gallery delivery workflow

The connected process can be understood in seven stages.

### 1. Confirm the booking

Check the client, job, package and expected deliverables before preparing the gallery.

### 2. Prepare the gallery

Upload the intended photographs, review the order and remove incomplete or duplicate files.

### 3. Share the gallery

Give the client one clear route to review the photographs and explain the action required.

### 4. Receive selections

Let the client select photographs in the gallery so the choices remain connected to the booking.

### 5. Review editing notes

Check the notes attached to the selection, clarify unclear requests and confirm what falls within scope.

### 6. Complete and verify the work

Edit the selected photographs, export the correct files and complete the studio's final quality checks.

### 7. Deliver and update the record

Deliver the finished photographs through the supported workflow, confirm receipt and keep the booking status accurate.

This sequence is deliberately simple. A small portrait studio may complete it quickly, while a wedding or corporate team may assign the stages to different people. The value comes from keeping every stage connected to the same client and job.

NDYLens gallery delivery can be especially useful when:

- selections arrive through several message threads
- editing notes are difficult to match to images
- team members cannot see whether the client has responded
- gallery delivery feels separate from the booking record
- the studio handles several active jobs at once
- past delivery history is difficult to find
- follow-up depends on someone remembering the client

A solo photographer can benefit from the structure, but the value becomes more visible as the number of clients, active galleries or team members grows.
