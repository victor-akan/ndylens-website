A reliable **client gallery delivery checklist** helps you catch small problems before they become a disappointing final impression. The photographs may be beautifully edited, but an incorrect password, missing image, unclear download option or copied client name can still make delivery feel careless.

Use this checklist after editing and export are complete, but before you send the gallery link. It covers the client-facing experience, the files, access settings, communication, record keeping and follow-up.

The aim is simple: the client should be able to open the gallery, understand what has been delivered, complete any required action and know where to get help.

## 1. Confirm the job and agreed deliverables

Start with the booking record, quotation or approved package. Do not rely on memory, especially when several jobs are moving through the studio at once.

Check that you have the correct:

- client name and contact details
- event or session name
- shoot date
- package and number of edited photographs
- promised delivery date
- additional images purchased after booking
- web-size or high-resolution versions included
- prints, frames, albums or other products still outstanding
- special privacy or sharing instructions

If a physical product or second set of files will arrive later, state that clearly in the delivery message. A client should not have to guess whether an item is delayed or forgotten.

## 2. Review every final image outside the editing catalogue

Exporting successfully does not prove that the delivery folder is correct. Review the actual exported files because that is what the client will receive.

Look for:

- duplicates and near-duplicates
- test exports or contact sheets
- missed retouching
- inconsistent exposure, colour or skin tone
- unexpected crops or orientation
- low-resolution previews mixed with final files
- broken or corrupted images
- filename changes that make selections hard to trace
- missing sections of the event or session

View the files at both thumbnail size and full size. Thumbnail review makes repetition and sequencing problems easier to spot. Full-size review helps reveal focus, retouching and export issues.

Where several people worked on the job, ask one person who did not prepare the export to perform the final review. A fresh pair of eyes often notices copied names, missing photographs and inconsistent versions more quickly.

## 3. Check the gallery title, cover and sequence

The gallery should identify the assignment without exposing more personal information than necessary.

Confirm that:

- the client-facing title is correct
- the cover image belongs to this job
- the opening photographs create a strong first impression
- sections follow a useful client-facing order
- the final image feels intentional
- no title, description or message was copied from another client

Use sections the client will understand. For example, a wedding gallery may use "Getting Ready," "Ceremony," "Portraits" and "Reception" rather than camera numbers or memory-card names.

Our guide to [naming and organising client galleries](/blog/how-to-name-and-organise-client-galleries/) explains how to keep client-facing names clear while maintaining a consistent internal archive.

## 4. Verify filenames and versions

Filenames are part of the client workflow when selections, re-edits or print orders are involved.

Before uploading, check that:

- filenames are unique
- numbering follows the intended sequence
- colour and black-and-white versions are clearly distinguished
- web-size and print-size versions use consistent suffixes
- filenames do not expose confusing internal notes
- the identifiers used during proofing still match the final files

Do not rename photographs after a client has started making selections unless you also update the selection record. If a client asks for image `IMG_0047`, everyone on the team should be able to identify the same photograph.

## 5. Match gallery controls to the package

The gallery settings should reflect what the client bought, not merely the software defaults.

Confirm the correct settings for:

- number of favourites or selections allowed
- ability to change selections after submission
- download access
- high-resolution and web-size options
- image purchasing, if offered
- comments or editing notes
- print ordering, if offered
- gallery expiry

If the package includes ten edited photographs, an unlimited selection control may create avoidable confusion. If downloads should remain disabled until the final balance is paid, check that the setting is actually enforced before delivery.

## 6. Test access as the client

An administrator view can hide problems. Open the exact client-facing link in a private browser window where you are not signed in to the gallery system.

Test the following:

- the link opens without a photographer account
- the password or access code works
- the correct gallery appears
- only the intended photographs are visible
- the cover image loads
- sections and navigation work
- favourites, comments or selections work as expected
- downloads produce the correct files
- the expiry information is accurate

Repeat the test on a phone. Many Nigerian clients will open the link directly from WhatsApp, often on mobile data. Confirm that the gallery loads at a practical speed, buttons are easy to tap and no part of the layout overflows the screen.

## 7. Confirm privacy and sharing rules

Privacy settings should match the assignment. A public event, corporate conference, newborn session and private portrait do not require the same controls.

Review whether the gallery should use:

- an unlisted link
- a password
- access limited to named people
- disabled downloads
- separate private sections
- an expiry date
- a public highlight gallery separate from the full delivery

Do not assume that a shareable link is private. A recipient may forward it or save the files. Explain what the client may share, and do not publish client photographs or feedback for marketing without the appropriate permission.

## 8. Test a real download

Previewing an image in the browser is not the same as downloading it.

Download at least one file from every available option. Then confirm:

- the file opens correctly
- its dimensions and quality are appropriate
- its filename is useful
- the colour looks consistent
- the download contains the expected number of files
- a ZIP archive opens without an error
- web-size and high-resolution options are clearly separated

If the client receives several folders, use simple names that describe their purpose. "For Printing" and "For WhatsApp and Social Media" are more useful than unexplained technical labels.

## 9. Make selection and editing instructions explicit

If the client must choose favourites or leave editing notes, the delivery message should answer these questions:

1. How many photographs may I select?
2. How do I mark a favourite?
3. Can another person contribute to the selection?
4. Where should editing notes be left?
5. Can selections be changed later?
6. What is the deadline?
7. When will the selected photographs be ready?

Choose one official route for selections and editing notes. If favourites are recorded in the gallery, avoid treating screenshots sent in one WhatsApp chat and a separate email list as equally final.

NDYLens supports client galleries where clients can select photographs and leave editing notes, keeping those choices connected to the client and booking workflow. That supported record reduces the risk of working from an outdated message thread.

## 10. Write a clear delivery message

The message should make the first action obvious. Keep the important details near the top.

A practical structure is:

1. Tell the client the gallery is ready.
2. Provide the link and password.
3. State what has been delivered.
4. Explain the next action.
5. Give the selection or expiry date.
6. Provide one support channel.

For example:

> Hi Ada, your birthday gallery is ready. You can open it with the private link below and use the access code provided. The gallery contains your 40 final edited photographs in high resolution. Please download them by 30 November 2026 and let me know here if you have any difficulty opening the files.

Avoid hiding the link and password inside several promotional paragraphs. The delivery message should first help the client receive the work.

## 11. Record the delivery before sending

Update the studio record while the details are still in front of you.

Record:

- the gallery URL
- delivery date and time
- access method
- selection deadline
- expiry date
- package and delivered file count
- privacy or sharing restrictions
- outstanding products or payments
- next follow-up date

This record helps another team member support the client without searching through old chats. It also gives the studio a clear history if the gallery must be restored or a selection question appears later.

## 12. Send and confirm the handoff

After all checks pass, send the exact link you tested. Do not copy a similar link from the gallery dashboard at the last moment without checking it again.

When appropriate, ask the client to confirm that the gallery opens and the files can be downloaded. If the client does not respond, follow up after a reasonable interval with a service-focused question rather than a sales message.

For example:

> Hi Ada, I wanted to confirm that the gallery opened correctly and that you could download the photographs. Please let me know if you need any help.

A successful delivery is not complete merely because the link was sent. The client must be able to receive and use the work.

## 13. Close the job properly

Once the client confirms receipt, complete the studio's closing steps:

- mark the gallery as delivered
- save the client's final selections and notes
- record any corrections or support provided
- confirm the status of prints, albums or frames
- schedule an expiry reminder if needed
- archive the final export according to studio policy
- retain client and booking information for future service and follow-up

## Printable client gallery delivery checklist

Use this compact version immediately before sending.

### Job and files

- Correct client, event, package and delivery date
- Agreed number of photographs included
- No unfinished edits, duplicates or test files
- Crops, colour, resolution and filenames checked
- Outstanding physical products clearly recorded

### Gallery presentation

- Correct title, description and cover photograph
- Images and sections arranged in a client-friendly order
- Final image and gallery ending reviewed
- No copied details from another job

### Access and privacy

- Client-facing link tested while signed out
- Password and permissions tested
- Mobile layout and loading checked
- Privacy and sharing settings match the assignment
- Expiry date and download rules are accurate

### Client actions

- Selection limit matches the package
- Favourites, comments and editing notes tested
- Download options produce the correct files
- Instructions, deadlines and next step are clear
- One support channel is provided

### Studio record

- Gallery URL and delivery date saved
- Job status updated
- Selections and editing notes stored together
- Follow-up and expiry reminders scheduled
- Final files archived according to policy

## Make consistency part of the client experience

A checklist is valuable because it makes quality less dependent on memory. It gives every client a consistent delivery experience, whether the job was a small portrait session, a wedding weekend or a large corporate event.

It also protects the studio. The team can see what was checked, what was delivered and what still needs attention.
