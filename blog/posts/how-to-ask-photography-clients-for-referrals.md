Think about your favourite clients from the past year. There’s a good chance some of them came through someone else: a bride who saw her friend’s wedding pictures, a mum in a school WhatsApp group asking “Who snapped your baby’s birthday?”, or a colleague who tagged you on Instagram.

Referrals are powerful, but most photographers leave them to chance. If you want a steadier flow of bookings, you need to ask photography clients for referrals on purpose, at the right moment and in a way that feels natural. This guide shows you how, with WhatsApp messages you can copy and a simple referral programme you can set up this week.

## Why referrals matter so much for Nigerian photographers

When someone chooses a photographer for a wedding, a child’s birthday or a maternity shoot, they’re trusting that person with a day they can’t repeat. A recommendation from a friend, sister or colleague takes away a lot of that worry.

A referred client usually arrives:

- **Already warm.** They’ve seen your work on someone they know.
- **With realistic expectations.** Their friend has told them roughly what you charge and how you work.
- **Ready to talk dates.** Less time convincing, more time planning.

Social circles here are closely connected, too. One wedding can put your work in front of a whole bridal train, several family WhatsApp groups and a hall full of guests. One happy client can lead to several enquiries, as long as you give them a reason and an easy way to mention you.

## When to ask clients for referrals

Timing matters more than wording. Ask when your client is feeling most positive about working with you.

**Good moments to ask:**

- Right after they react happily to their preview or final gallery
- When they post your pictures and tag you
- When they send a thank-you message or voice note
- When they book you again, which shows they already trust you

**Moments to avoid:**

- Before you’ve delivered their pictures
- While there’s an outstanding balance or a disagreement about edits
- In the middle of a busy, stressful event day

If a client writes “These pictures are everything!”, that’s your cue.

## How to ask for referrals without feeling awkward

Many photographers avoid asking because it feels like begging. It isn’t. You’re giving a happy client a simple way to help someone they know find a photographer they can trust.

### Be specific

“Please refer me” is easy to agree with and just as easy to forget. It works much better to say who you’re hoping to meet. A bride probably knows other people getting married soon. A new mum probably knows other expectant mums.

### Make it easy to share

Give them something they can forward in one tap: a link to your portfolio, your Instagram handle or a short message they can pass on.

### Ask once, then say thank you

One clear, warm request is enough. Repeating it every few weeks turns goodwill into irritation.

## WhatsApp templates for asking for referrals

Copy these and adjust the wording until it sounds like you.

**After delivering the final gallery:**

> Hi [Name], I’m so glad you love your pictures! Thank you for trusting us with your [occasion]. If you know anyone planning a [wedding / birthday / maternity shoot] soon, I’d be really grateful if you shared my details with them. Here’s a link to my work: [portfolio link]

**When a client posts your pictures and tags you:**

> Thank you so much for sharing the pictures, [Name]! Seeing them posted made our week. If any of your friends ask who took them, please feel free to send them my way.

**For a bride whose friends are getting married:**

> Hi [Name], I hope married life is treating you well! Working on your wedding was such a joy. If any of your friends have weddings coming up, I’d love to capture their day too. Feel free to forward them the message below.

**For a maternity or newborn client:**

> Hi [Name], I hope you and [baby’s name] are doing well. If you know any expectant mums who’d love maternity or newborn pictures, I’d be grateful if you passed my number along. Thank you for your support!

**A message your client can forward:**

> Hi! I wanted to recommend [Studio name], who photographed our [occasion]. You can see their work here: [portfolio link]. Message them on WhatsApp at [number] to check dates and packages.

Writing the forwardable message yourself means your client doesn’t have to think about what to say, and the person receiving it gets your details in the right format.

## Setting up a simple photography referral programme in Nigeria

A referral programme turns occasional word of mouth into a habit. It doesn’t need to be complicated. You need three things: a reward, clear rules and a way to keep track.

### Choose a reward that makes sense

Pick something that feels generous to the client without eating into your profit. Options include:

- A discount on their next session, for example ₦10,000 off
- Extra edited pictures from their existing gallery
- A short mini-session for their family
- A framed print of their favourite picture

Rewards that mainly cost you time, like extra edits or a mini-session, are often easier to sustain than cash discounts. You could also offer the new client a small welcome perk, so both sides benefit.

### Set clear rules

Decide in advance:

- **When the reward is earned.** For example, once the referred client pays their deposit, not just when they send an enquiry.
- **How long it lasts.** A reward that expires after six or twelve months encourages people to use it.
- **Whether there’s a limit.** Can one client earn more than one reward?

Write the rules down so you apply them the same way every time.

### Tell your clients about it

> Hi [Name]! We’ve started something special for our past clients: whenever someone you refer books a shoot with us, you’ll get [reward] on your next session. Just ask them to mention your name when they message us. Thank you for always supporting us!

Send this personally to clients who’ve had a great experience with you, rather than as a blanket announcement. It lands better when it feels like an invitation.

## Track every referral

A referral programme only works if you know where your bookings come from. Ask every new enquiry:

> Thank you for reaching out! Before I send our packages, may I ask how you heard about us?

Then record the answer alongside the new client’s details: who referred them, when they booked and whether the reward has been given. A simple spreadsheet is enough. What matters is that you never forget to thank the person who sent them.

When a referral books, let the referrer know straight away:

> Hi [Name], I just wanted to say a big thank you! [New client’s name] has booked a shoot with us and mentioned you. Your [reward] is ready whenever you’d like to use it.

Only mention the new client’s name if they’re comfortable with it. If you’re not sure, simply say “someone you referred”.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Asking before you’ve earned it.** Deliver great pictures first.
- **Being vague.** “Tell your friends about me” rarely leads anywhere.
- **Forgetting to say thank you.** A referrer who’s never acknowledged is unlikely to do it again.
- **Promising rewards you don’t track.** If you offer something, record it and honour it.
- **Only asking recent clients.** People you photographed two or three years ago can still send clients your way, especially if you’ve kept in touch.

## Referrals grow from relationships you keep

The clients most likely to recommend you are the ones who still remember you. Someone you photographed once and never contacted again may struggle to recall your studio name when a friend asks. Someone you messaged ahead of their anniversary or their child’s birthday won’t.
