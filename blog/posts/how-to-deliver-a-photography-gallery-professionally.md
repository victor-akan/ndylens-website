A photography gallery is more than a folder of finished files. It is the final presentation of the work a client trusted you to create.

The photographs may be excellent, but the delivery can still feel careless if the link arrives without context, the gallery is difficult to navigate, the files are incorrectly sized or the client does not know what to do next. A professional photography gallery delivery process removes that uncertainty.

This guide explains how to prepare, present and deliver a client gallery in a way that feels organised, protects the work and makes the experience easier for both the photographer and the client.

## What professional gallery delivery should achieve

A strong delivery process should help the client answer five questions quickly:

1. Where are my photographs?
2. How do I view and download them?
3. What am I allowed to do with them?
4. Is there anything I still need to select or approve?
5. Who should I contact if I need help?

The process should also help the studio confirm that the correct files reached the correct client, that any remaining selections or editing notes are visible and that the job can move towards completion.

Professional delivery does not require an elaborate presentation for every shoot. It requires a clear, repeatable system appropriate to the service you sold.

## 1. Confirm the agreed deliverables before exporting

Return to the quotation, package, invoice or booking agreement before you prepare the final gallery. Do not rely on memory, especially when several jobs are moving through the studio at once.

Check:

- The number of edited photographs included
- Whether the client receives high-resolution files, web-sized files or both
- Whether there are black-and-white versions
- Whether prints, albums or frames are included
- The agreed delivery date or turnaround period
- Whether the client must select favourites first
- Whether additional retouching or extra images have been purchased
- Any private images that need restricted access

This check prevents two common problems: delivering less than the client expected and giving away work that was not part of the agreed package.

If the package changed after the original booking, make sure the latest approved version is reflected in the delivery record.

## 2. Complete a final image quality check

Review the exported files, not only the photographs inside your editing software. An image can look correct in the working catalogue but export with the wrong crop, colour profile, filename or resolution.

### Check image quality

Open a representative sample at full size and inspect:

- Exposure and colour consistency
- Skin tones
- Sharpening and noise
- Crops and straight horizons
- Retouching around faces, clothing and backgrounds
- Visible sensor dust or distracting objects
- Export resolution and compression

For an important event, review the full set in sequence. Look for accidental duplicates, missing moments and sudden changes in colour or orientation.

### Check the client and event details

Confirm that the gallery name, client name and event date are correct. When studios handle similar weddings, birthdays or corporate sessions in the same week, a careful identity check is essential.

Never assume that a familiar-looking folder is the right one. Match the files to the booking record and, where possible, use a second person or a formal checklist for final approval.

### Check filenames

Use filenames that remain useful after download. A consistent pattern might include the studio name, client or event reference and sequence number.

Avoid exposing internal notes, rejected-edit labels or confusing camera filenames if they will make the delivered set harder to understand. Keep the naming system simple enough for the client to locate and share a particular image.

## 3. Organise the gallery for the way the client will use it

A gallery should not force the client to make sense of your production folders. Organise it around their experience.

For a wedding, useful sections might include:

- Preparation
- Ceremony
- Couple portraits
- Family and wedding party
- Reception
- Details

For a brand or corporate shoot, sections may be better organised by location, outfit, product, team or intended use.

Keep the number of sections reasonable. Too many folders can make a small gallery feel fragmented, while one very long stream can make a large event difficult to browse.

Choose a strong cover photograph that represents the session and looks good across desktop and mobile displays. The cover should create a confident first impression without revealing an image the client asked to keep private.

## 4. Set the right access and download options

Before sending the link, review the gallery settings from the client's point of view.

### Privacy

Use an appropriate password or private access method when the photographs are personal, confidential or not intended for public discovery. Send access information through a suitable channel, and avoid placing a sensitive password in a public post.

For events involving children, private family moments, unreleased products or confidential corporate work, confirm the studio's consent and privacy obligations before enabling sharing.

### Downloads

Decide which download options match the agreement:

- High-resolution files for printing
- Web-sized files for online use
- Individual image downloads
- Full-gallery downloads
- Downloads disabled until selection or payment is complete, if this was agreed in advance

Do not introduce a new restriction at delivery. If final download depends on selection, approval or an outstanding payment, that condition should already be clear in the client's agreement and earlier communication.

### Expiry and retention

Tell the client whether the gallery expires and what happens afterwards. Give enough time for viewing and downloading, and send a reminder before access ends when appropriate.

An online gallery should not be the client's only permanent backup. Likewise, the studio needs its own documented backup and retention process. Be specific about how long files are retained instead of promising indefinite storage unless you can genuinely provide it.

## 5. Test the gallery as a client

Open the delivery link in a private browser window or on a device that is not already signed into the studio account. This reveals problems that may be hidden when you view the gallery as its owner.

Test:

- The link opens without an error
- The password or access code works
- The correct cover and gallery title appear
- Images load on mobile and desktop
- Sections appear in the right order
- Favourites or selections work, when enabled
- Editing notes can be submitted, when enabled
- Download buttons match the package
- A sample download opens correctly
- Contact or help information is visible

Where internet connections may be inconsistent, avoid making the client load unnecessarily large previews just to browse. Test the experience on a mobile connection as well as reliable Wi-Fi.

## 6. Write a delivery message that guides the client

Do not send a bare link. A short delivery message should celebrate the moment and explain the next action.

A practical message can include:

- A personal greeting
- Confirmation that the gallery is ready
- The secure link and access details
- Simple viewing and download instructions
- Any selection or approval deadline
- The gallery expiry date, if applicable
- A reminder to save copies
- How to request help
- What happens after the client responds

For example:

> Hello [client name], your gallery is ready. Thank you again for trusting us to photograph [event or session]. You can view it here: [gallery link]. Use [access method] to open it. Please select your [number] favourites by [date], and reply here if you have any difficulty. The gallery will remain available until [date], so please download and back up your final photographs before then.

Adapt the wording to the actual service. Do not mention a selection deadline, expiry date or included quantity unless it is accurate for that booking.

## 7. Make selections and editing notes easy to track

Some galleries are final deliveries. Others are part of a selection and revision stage.

If the client must choose photographs, state:

- How many selections are included
- How to mark favourites
- The deadline for submitting them
- Whether extra selections cost more
- What happens after the selections are received

If editing notes are allowed, ask the client to attach each note to the relevant image rather than sending a separate list of vague descriptions. A note such as “reduce the reflection on image 014” is easier to act on than “fix the photo by the window.”

Keep approvals and requested changes connected to the client and booking. This is one reason a [photography CRM workflow from enquiry to delivery](/blog/a-simple-photography-crm-workflow-from-enquiry-to-delivery/) can be more reliable than searching through several chats and folders.

Set boundaries around revision rounds and the kind of editing included. Clear boundaries protect the schedule while helping the client understand what to expect.

## 8. Confirm payment status without creating surprises

Gallery delivery and payment often meet at the final stage of a job. The process should follow the terms already agreed with the client.

Before delivery, check the payment record and confirm whether:

- The package has been paid in full
- A final balance is due before high-resolution download
- An album, print or additional edit requires a separate payment
- A verified payment has not yet been matched to the booking

Never rely only on a transfer screenshot. Confirm funds through the relevant bank or payment provider records.

If a balance is due, communicate politely and precisely. State the amount, the agreed due date and the delivery step connected to it. ## 9. Record delivery and follow up

Sending the link is not always the same as completing the job. Record the delivery date and the current status.

Useful statuses include:

- Gallery prepared
- Quality check complete
- Gallery sent
- Client viewed
- Selection pending
- Revisions in progress
- Final files delivered
- Delivery confirmed
- Job complete

Not every platform will show when a client has viewed the gallery, and a view does not prove that the files were downloaded safely. Use a brief follow-up when confirmation matters.

A simple message might be:

> Hello [client name], I wanted to confirm that you could open and download your gallery successfully. Please let me know if you need any help before the gallery closes on [date].

After the client confirms delivery, record any remaining album, print or revision work. Then move the booking to its completed stage instead of leaving it mixed with active editing jobs.

## 10. Continue the relationship respectfully

Delivery can become the beginning of the next stage of the client relationship, but the immediate priority is a good conclusion to the current job.

Once the client has received the work, you may appropriately:

- Thank them for the booking
- Ask for feedback
- Request a review at a sensible time
- Explain print or album options
- Ask for permission before publishing private photographs
- Record relevant future dates with consent
- Plan a useful follow-up based on the service

Avoid asking for a review before the client has had a fair chance to view the photographs. Do not combine delivery with a long sales message that distracts from the moment.

## A professional photography gallery delivery checklist

Use this checklist before you send any client gallery.

### Files and quality

- [ ] Deliverables match the current agreement
- [ ] Final exports have been reviewed
- [ ] Client, event and folder details are correct
- [ ] Duplicates, missing files and accidental rejects have been checked
- [ ] Resolution, colour and filenames are appropriate

### Gallery setup

- [ ] Cover photograph and gallery title are correct
- [ ] Sections are organised for the client
- [ ] Privacy and password settings are appropriate
- [ ] Download options match the package
- [ ] Selection and editing-note tools work, if needed
- [ ] Expiry and retention information is accurate

### Testing

- [ ] Link opens in a private browser window
- [ ] Mobile and desktop views have been tested
- [ ] Access details work
- [ ] A sample file downloads and opens correctly
- [ ] Client help information is visible

### Communication and records

- [ ] Delivery message explains the next action
- [ ] Payment status has been verified
- [ ] Selection, revision or expiry deadlines are included
- [ ] Delivery date and status are recorded
- [ ] Follow-up is scheduled where appropriate

## Common gallery delivery mistakes to avoid

### Sending the wrong link

Copy the link from the final client-facing gallery and test it. Internal editing or preview links may behave differently.

### Delivering without context

A client should not have to guess how to download, select or respond. Add brief instructions.

### Leaving every setting at its default

Review privacy, downloads, expiry and sharing for each kind of job. One default may not suit both a private family session and a public corporate event.

### Promising permanent storage

Only promise what your storage and retention process can support. Encourage clients to create their own backups.

### Mixing feedback across channels

When selections arrive in the gallery, editing notes arrive on WhatsApp and approvals arrive by email, the studio can easily miss a request. Define one method for each stage and connect it to the booking record.

### Marking a job complete too early

A sent link may still be waiting for selections, revisions, final downloads, an album or a balance. Use a clear completion rule.

## Build delivery into the client journey

Professional photography gallery delivery is a sequence, not a single link. Confirm the agreement, review the exports, organise the presentation, test the client experience, guide the recipient and record what happens next.

When this process is repeatable, clients receive a clearer experience and the studio spends less time resolving avoidable confusion. It also creates a reliable handover between shooting, editing, delivery, payment and future follow-up.
