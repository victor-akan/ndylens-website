The best way to send photos to clients without losing quality is to deliver the exported files through a dedicated online gallery or a reliable cloud link, then give the client a clear option to download the original-resolution files.

Email attachments and images sent directly through messaging apps are convenient for previews, but they are not dependable as the master delivery method. File-size limits, automatic compression and confusing download steps can reduce image quality or leave clients unsure about which version to use.

A professional delivery process protects more than pixels. It helps the client receive the correct files, understand the available sizes and keep a usable copy after the project is complete.

## What “losing quality” actually means

Image quality can be reduced at several points between export and download. Knowing where the loss happens makes it easier to prevent.

### Resolution is reduced

Resolution describes the pixel dimensions of an image. A photograph exported at 6000 by 4000 pixels contains much more image data than a 1600 by 1067 pixel copy. The smaller file may look excellent on a phone, but it allows less cropping and may not support a large print.

### Compression removes image data

JPEG compression makes files smaller. Moderate compression is useful and can be visually difficult to notice, but aggressive or repeated compression may soften fine detail, create blocky edges and produce visible banding in smooth tones.

A JPEG does not automatically become lower quality simply because it travels through the internet. Quality is lost when the file is resized, recompressed or converted during export, upload, transfer or download.

### The colour profile changes

Colour can also appear wrong even when the pixel dimensions stay the same. An image exported in a wide-gamut colour space may look dull or inconsistent in software that does not manage colour correctly. For general client viewing and common print services, sRGB is usually the most compatible export choice.

### The client saves a preview instead of the original

A client may take a screenshot, save a social-media preview or long-press a thumbnail instead of using the gallery's download control. The delivery system may hold the full-quality file while the client unknowingly keeps a smaller copy.

This is why clear instructions matter as much as the transfer platform.

## The best delivery setup for most photographers

For most professional assignments, use a two-version workflow:

1. Export a high-resolution set for archiving and printing.
2. Export a smaller web-ready set for WhatsApp, Instagram, email and fast everyday sharing.
3. Upload both to a client gallery or structured cloud folder.
4. Label the download choices in plain language.
5. Send one delivery message with the link, access details, download guidance and availability period.

This gives the client convenience without making the convenient copy the only copy.

If your gallery platform can generate web-sized downloads from the master files, you may only need to upload one high-quality set. Test the actual client download before relying on that behaviour.

## Choose the right way to send the files

No single transfer method is perfect for every assignment. Choose according to the number of files, total size, privacy needs and experience promised in the package.

### Dedicated online photo gallery

An online gallery is often the best client-facing option for weddings, portraits, events and other multi-image assignments. It lets clients view a curated sequence before downloading and can make separate download sizes easier to understand.

Look for practical controls such as:

- Original-resolution downloads
- Web-size downloads
- Password or access protection
- Gallery expiry settings
- Mobile-friendly viewing
- Download tracking, if your process needs it
- Clear control over whether visitors can share or order products

Do not assume every gallery service preserves originals in the same way. Review its upload processing and download settings, then test a delivered file against your export.

### Cloud storage link

A well-organised cloud folder can work for commercial projects, internal company teams and clients who mainly need files rather than a presentation. It is also useful when the deliverables include several formats or supporting documents.

Use separate folders such as `High Resolution`, `Web and Social`, and `Print Release` where appropriate. Avoid sending a link to a cluttered working folder that contains previews, rejected images or ambiguous versions.

Check the sharing permission in a private browser window before delivery. A beautiful export is not useful if the client cannot open it or if the wrong people can access it.

### File-transfer service

A transfer service is useful for a large one-time package when the client is comfortable downloading an archive. It can preserve the uploaded files, but the experience may feel temporary and less browsable than a gallery.

Tell the client when the link expires and encourage them to save the complete package promptly. Keep your own backup according to the retention terms you communicated.

### Email attachments

Email is suitable for a small preview, a contact sheet or a handful of modestly sized images. It is usually poor for a complete high-resolution gallery because attachment limits split the delivery across messages and make version control difficult.

Use email to send the gallery link and instructions rather than forcing the full job into attachments.

### WhatsApp and other messaging apps

Messaging is helpful for sending a delivery notice, a link or a few web-ready highlights. It should not be your only route for master files unless you have verified the app's current document-transfer behaviour and the client understands exactly how to download the original file.

When speed matters, send the gallery link through the channel the client already uses. Keep the master delivery in a system designed to preserve and organise the files.

## Export high-resolution files correctly

Good delivery begins before the upload. Work from the final edited masters and use an export preset you have tested.

### Use appropriate pixel dimensions

If the agreement promises full-resolution files, export at the camera crop's finished pixel dimensions unless a specific print requirement says otherwise. Do not enlarge files simply to create a bigger number. Upscaling cannot restore detail that the original capture does not contain.

For web-ready copies, choose dimensions that balance appearance and transfer speed. The right number depends on the use, so describe these files as suitable for online sharing rather than promising that one dimension fits every platform forever.

### Choose a sensible JPEG quality

A high-quality JPEG is normally practical for client photographs. The maximum quality setting can create substantially larger files without a meaningful visible improvement in ordinary use. A carefully tested high setting is often the better balance.

There is no universal percentage because export tools use different quality scales. Test your own preset on skin, fabric, foliage, gradients and low-light detail. Inspect the exported file at 100 percent and compare it with the edited master.

### Export in sRGB for broad compatibility

sRGB is the safest general-purpose colour profile for client screens, browsers, social platforms and many consumer print services. If a professional printer requests another profile, create that version separately and label it clearly.

### Apply sharpening for the intended output

Output sharpening should match the final use. Web images and print files may need different treatment. Avoid stacking strong export sharpening on top of an already oversharpened edit.

### Keep useful metadata and remove sensitive data

Copyright and creator information can be useful. Location metadata may not be appropriate for every client, especially for private homes, children or sensitive assignments. Decide deliberately which metadata remains in the delivered file.

## Name and organise the files clearly

Clients should not need to decode your editing process. Use filenames that remain useful after the images leave your system.

A consistent pattern might include the client or project, event date and sequence number:

`Amina-Kunle-Wedding-2026-10-03-001.jpg`

Use leading zeros so files sort correctly. Avoid unexplained camera filenames when several photographers contributed to the job, and do not include words such as `final-final-2`.

If you provide more than one size, keep the same base filename in clearly named folders. This reduces the chance that the client prints a small social copy or uploads a large print file where a smaller version would be easier.

## Verify the files after upload

An upload confirmation is not a delivery check. Open the client-facing link and test what the client will actually receive.

Use this quality-control sequence:

1. Open the link in a private browser window or logged-out session.
2. Confirm the gallery or folder name and client identity.
3. Check that the image count matches your approved export.
4. Download at least one landscape image, one portrait image and one detailed or low-light image.
5. Compare pixel dimensions, file size, filename and colour appearance with the exported originals.
6. Test the password, expiry date and sharing permission.
7. Open the link on a phone as well as a computer.
8. Confirm that no working files, duplicates or private images are visible.

For high-value work, use checksums or another file-integrity method when moving masters between storage systems. For routine client delivery, a disciplined sample download and comparison catches many practical errors.

## Tell the client which download to choose

Do not send a bare link. A short message can prevent screenshots, repeated questions and poor-quality prints.

Explain:

- What the gallery contains
- Whether a password or email sign-in is required
- Which option is best for printing
- Which option is best for WhatsApp or social media
- Whether the client must make selections
- When the link will expire
- How long you retain a backup, if that is part of your terms
- How to contact the studio for help

For example:

> Your finished gallery is ready. Use “High Resolution” for printing and safekeeping, and “Web Size” for WhatsApp or social media. Please download and back up the complete gallery before the stated expiry date. Reply here if you have any trouble accessing the files.

Adjust the wording to match your real service and platform. Never promise indefinite storage unless your business genuinely provides it.

## Protect privacy and access

Quality is not the only delivery risk. Client photographs can contain personal, commercial or sensitive information.

Use access protection appropriate to the assignment. A family portrait gallery, private event and unreleased corporate campaign may require different controls. Send passwords through a suitable channel, avoid public links where privacy is expected and remove access when the agreed period ends.

Only collect the client information your workflow needs. If several team members handle delivery, make responsibilities and access permissions clear.

## Keep a dependable backup before and after delivery

Do not treat a live gallery as the only copy of the work. Keep the finished masters in a separate, controlled backup system before sending the link.

A practical approach keeps:

- The working files while editing is active
- A local or primary copy of the final exports
- A second copy on separate storage
- An off-site or cloud backup appropriate to the business

Tell clients how long you retain delivered files and follow that policy consistently. The client should still be encouraged to make their own backup after download.

## A final checklist for lossless-looking client delivery

Before you send the link, confirm that:

- The files match the agreed deliverables
- High-resolution exports have the intended pixel dimensions
- The colour profile is suitable for the client's use
- JPEG quality has been tested on real photographs
- Web and print versions are clearly separated
- Filenames and gallery details identify the correct job
- A downloaded sample matches the uploaded export
- Permissions, password and expiry are correct
- The message explains how to download each version
- A separate studio backup exists
- The delivery is recorded against the right client and booking

The best way to send photos to clients without losing quality is not simply to pick the biggest file or the newest platform. It is to control the export, use a transfer method that preserves the intended files, test the result and guide the client towards the correct download.

That combination protects the photographs and makes the final stage of the client experience feel as considered as the shoot itself.
